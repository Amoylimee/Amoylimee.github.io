Does AIS temporal resolution matter? Assessing point-based trajectory thinning biases in shipping emission inventories With G. Li, D. Liang, Y. Chen, Y. Zhang, H. Lei, J. W. M. Chan, and A. K. H. Lau Working paper

High-frequency Automatic Identification System data are often thinned to reduce the computational cost of building shipping emission inventories, but the resulting bias is not well understood. Using records from more than 5,000 vessels in China’s Greater Bay Area, this study quantifies how temporal subsampling affects emission estimates across vessel classes and traces those errors into air quality simulations. The results show systematic, interval-dependent emission underestimation, driven primarily by lost reconstructed activity time. Although regional-average concentration changes are modest, localized underestimation of NOx and SO2 can reach approximately 40% near ports and shipping fairways, potentially obscuring pollution hotspots relevant to exposure assessment and coastal mitigation.

TANUF: A type-aware neural uncertainty framework for AIS-based port emissions inventories With H. Lei, S. Jia, and A. K. H. Lau Working paper

This research develops an uncertainty-aware approach for estimating port emissions when vessel information derived from Automatic Identification System data is incomplete. It combines type-aware vessel inference with probabilistic estimation to improve the treatment of missing vessel characteristics and avoid false precision.

Red Sea rerouting shifted cyclone exposure in global trade With X. Chen and A. K. H. Lau Working paper

This study examines how the 2023 Red Sea disruption shifted shipping from the Suez Canal towards the Cape of Good Hope, changing voyage duration, direct services, cyclone exposure, and the distribution of trade losses. It links AIS trajectories, tropical-cyclone records, bilateral trade, and input-output data.

Vessel traffic flow prediction for smart port logistics: A hierarchical adaptive spatio-temporal transformer H. Lei, J. Chen, H. Li, S. Jia*, and X. Wang Transportation Research Part E: Logistics and Transportation Review, 215, 105089, 2026

Reliable vessel traffic flow prediction is essential for proactive port management, but real-world applications are challenged by heterogeneous inputs, noisy multi-scale dynamics, rapidly changing port-network structures, and sparse observations. This study develops a Hierarchical Adaptive Spatio-Temporal Transformer (HAST-Former) that integrates feature alignment, multi-scale temporal guidance, adaptive spatial graph convolution, and spatio-temporal self-attention. Evaluations using AIS-derived data from Shanghai and Zhoushan show consistent improvements over leading benchmarks, including stronger performance in high-variance and long-horizon scenarios. The framework provides a robust basis for vessel traffic forecasting and data-driven smart port logistics.

Vessel traffic flow prediction through multi-scale spatiotemporal attention in dual-graph networks H. Lei, R. Liu, J. Chen*, H. Li, and S. Jia Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies, 184, 105529, 2026

Accurate vessel traffic flow forecasting is complicated by interacting spatial and temporal patterns and by both deterministic and stochastic influences. This study develops a Parallel Spatiotemporal Attention framework that combines multi-view temporal convolutional networks, long short-term memory units, and dual graphs representing geographic proximity and functional similarity. A constraint-aware cross-attention mechanism incorporates waterway connectivity and Automatic Identification System data quality. Cross-port experiments in Zhoushan and Shanghai show that the framework consistently outperforms leading benchmarks and can support data-driven maritime traffic management.

Multi-criteria optimization of methanol-based energy systems in ports using PMPEF-TOPSIS: A path to sustainable and efficient operations H. Lei*, X. Ren, J. Chen, and Y. Ma Journal of Cleaner Production, 538, 147366, 2026

Ports are strategic, energy-intensive nodes in global logistics, and decarbonization pressures are driving the need for low-carbon, infrastructure-compatible solutions. Methanol offers a technically practical pathway due to its liquid form, established handling compatibility, and lower emission potential relative to conventional fuels. This study presents the first structured comparative evaluation of methanol-based port power systems across thermochemical, electrochemical, and hydrogen-carrier routes. A new multi-criteria decision-making model, the Port Methanol Power System Evaluation Framework–TOPSIS (PMPEF-TOPSIS), is developed to assess feasibility across technical, economic, environmental, and regulatory dimensions. The TOPSIS results indicate substantial differentiation in criterion influence. Technological Innovation (1.000) and Environmental Impact (0.941) exhibit the highest proximity to the ideal solution, followed by Cost Efficiency (0.735), demonstrating that technology advancement and emissions performance remain the primary leverage points for methanol system feasibility. Performance in Dynamic Scenarios (0.000) and Economic Feasibility (0.382) ranked lowest, highlighting current limitations in operational robustness and macro-economic resilience. Electrochemical conversion ranked first among the alternatives (0.73), indicating the strongest overall suitability for port deployment. This framework offers a robust basis for strategic prioritisation and investment sequencing in port energy transition planning.
