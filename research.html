Research

Maritime systems, emissions, and the low-carbon transition

My research combines shipping data, quantitative modelling, and policy analysis. The central aim is to produce evidence that is technically credible, transparent about uncertainty, and useful for decisions by ports, policymakers, and the maritime industry.

Working papers

Working paper Shipping emissions · Machine learning

TANUF: A Type-Aware Neural Uncertainty Framework for AIS-Based Port Emissions Inventories

This research develops an uncertainty-aware approach for estimating port emissions when vessel information derived from Automatic Identification System data is incomplete. It combines type-aware vessel inference with probabilistic estimation to improve the treatment of missing vessel characteristics and to avoid presenting uncertain emissions estimates with false precision.

Manuscript AIS data · Emissions estimation

Temporal Resolution in AIS-Based Shipping Emissions Estimation

This study examines how the temporal resolution of vessel activity data influences shipping emissions estimates. It evaluates when lower-frequency observations preserve operational patterns and when they introduce material bias, with the aim of supporting more transparent and computationally efficient emissions inventories.

Policy and applied research

Green ports and maritime fuel transition

Policy-facing research on port emissions, green maritime fuels, infrastructure readiness, and the practical conditions needed to support fuel transition in Hong Kong and regional shipping.

Vessel retrofit finance and incentives

Analysis of shipowners’ technology investment decisions, financing constraints, and the design of incentives for vessel retrofitting and energy-efficiency measures.

Green shipping corridors

Research on how route-level partnerships, fuel supply, vessel-side readiness, and policy coordination can translate decarbonisation ambitions into implementable corridor plans.
